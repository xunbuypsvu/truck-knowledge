# Garbage Truck 1980S - Technical Overview and Buying Tips

> 垃圾车 1980s

The garbage truck 1980s is typically configured with a power take-off (PTO) system driving the specialized equipment. Engine options range from 180 to 420 hp depending on capacity requirements, with 6-speed to 12-speed manual or AMT gearboxes. Emission standards range from Euro 3 to Euro 6 depending on the destination market's requirements.

Applications for the garbage truck 1980s span municipal services, construction sites, industrial facilities and agricultural operations. In African markets, government and NGO procurement drives significant demand, while in Southeast Asia the private construction sector is the primary buyer. Latin American buyers increasingly source directly from Chinese factories to bypass import markup layers.

Proper maintenance of a garbage truck 1980s centers on the PTO engagement system, hydraulic oil quality, and body mounting integrity. Most Chinese export models are designed for simple field maintenance with standard tools. A complete spare parts kit (filters, seals, hydraulic hoses) should be ordered with the initial purchase to ensure availability in remote operating locations.

## FAQ

**Q: how to replace hydraulic hose on dump trailer**

A special truck operates on a standard truck chassis with a power take-off (PTO) driving the specialized equipment: hydraulics raise, lower or drive the working body while the operator controls the cycle from the cab or a panel. Daily checks cover PTO engagement, hydraulic oil level and body mounting bolts. Export units usually run a 260 hp diesel engine matched to the body's demand, keeping fuel burn predictable at roughly 18-25 L/100 km depending on load.

**Q: is trash pickup cancelled tomorrow**

Start with three numbers: daily workload, required capacity and road conditions. Then pick the chassis — 4x2 for city routes, 6x4 or 8x4 for heavy loads and rough sites. For a special truck, a 260 hp engine with a special purpose body is the common export configuration. Verify emission compliance for your country (Euro 3/4/5 for most African and Asian markets), ask for factory inspection photos before container loading, and reserve 10-15% of the truck price for first-year spare parts.
