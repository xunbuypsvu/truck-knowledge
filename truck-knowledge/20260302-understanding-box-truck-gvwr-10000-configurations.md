## Field Notes

Typical chassis options include 4x2 light-duty and medium-duty trucks with payloads from about 3 to 10 tons. Standard box bodies are often 14 to 28 cubic meters, while refrigerated versions usually lose some internal volume because of insulation and cooling equipment. Buyers often see a 7.5-ton box truck with a 4.2 m to 5.2 m body length as the most versatile option for urban delivery and intercity distribution. For refrigerated use, reefer units with -18 C to +5 C capability are common for frozen food and dairy.

TCO is where experienced buyers separate profitable fleets from expensive ones. Real-world operating cost includes fuel, tire wear, insurance, permits, driver behavior, body repairs, and temperature-control power use for reefer models. In hot climates, a poorly insulated refrigerated box truck can burn noticeably more fuel because the unit cycles longer and harder. Over three years, that can erase the savings from a lower purchase price.
